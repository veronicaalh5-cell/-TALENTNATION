Algorithm Thinking -- Simple Project Answers

Tier 1: Giving Clear Instructions

Project: Making Breakfast

I would prepare fried eggs and toasted bread for one person.


Things I need 

- 2 eggs  
- 2 slices of bread 
- A little cooking oil
- A pinch of salt 
- A frying pan,
- spatula, 
- toaster 
- plate

Steps 

1. Wash my hands and bring out all the ingredients. 
2. Put thefrying pan on the stove. 
3. Add a little oil and heat it.
4. Break the eggs into a bowl and check that no eggshells are inside. 
5. Pour the eggs into the pan and add a little salt. 
6. Fry until the eggs are fullycooked. 
7. Toast the two slices of bread. 
8. Put the eggs and bread on aplate. 
9. Switch off the stove and serve the food.

What was missing from "Make me breakfast"?

The instruction did not say what food to make, how much to prepare,
where to find the ingredients, what equipment to use or where to serve
it. I had to decide these details before I could give clear steps.

What I learned

Instructions should be clear and arranged in the right order. This helps
someone complete a task without guessing.


Tier 2: Algorithm Thinking


Project: Withdrawing Money from an ATM

What is needed?
- An ATM card 
- The correct PIN 
- A working ATM 
- Enough money in the bank account

Steps 1. Insert the ATM card. 

2. Enter the PIN. 
3. Choose "Withdrawal". 
4. Select the account type. 
5. Enter the amount.
6. Check that the account has enough money. 
7. Confirm the transaction. 
8. Collect the cash. 
9. Collect the card and receipt, if requested.



What if something goes wrong? - If the PIN is wrong, try again. Stop
if the ATM blocks the card or the allowed attempts are used. - If there
is not enough money, enter a smaller amount or cancel. - If the ATM has
no cash, cancel or try another ATM. - If money is deducted but no cash
comes out, keep the transaction details and contact the bank.


What I learned


An algorithm is a set of steps used to solve a problem. The steps must
be in the correct order and should explain what to do when something
goes wrong.


The difference - Problem: What needs to be solved. 

-Algorithm: The steps for solving it. 
- Program: The instructions written in a programming language.


Tier 3: Input, Processing and Output


Project: A Simple Calculator
A calculator takes two numbers, performs a calculation and shows the
answer.



For example, using 20 and 5:


Operation          Calculation   Answer
Addition                20 + 5       25
Subtraction             20 - 5       15
Multiplication          20 × 5      100
Division                20 ÷ 5        4



If someone tries to divide 20 by 0, the calculator should show an error
because division by zero is not allowed.


The three stages 

- Input: The numbers entered (20 and 5). 
- Processing: The calculation carried out. 
- Output: The answer shown on the screen.



What I learned


A computer receives information, works on it and gives a result. This is
called input, processing and output.


Tier 4: Understanding Data


Project: Different Types of Data


A computer stores different kinds of information. For example, a bank
may store these details about a customer:

Information       Example     Data type
Customer name     Victor      String (text)
Age               30          Integer (whole number)
Account balance   150000.50   Decimal number
Account active    True        Boolean (true or false)


Simple explanations 

String: Text, such as a name or address.
Integer: A whole number, such as 30.
Decimal: A number with a decimal point, such as 150000.50.
Boolean: A value that is either true or false.


Computers store information using binary, which uses only 0 and 1. Text
and numbers are changed into a form the computer can store and work
with.


Why correct data matters


If a bank records ₦150,000.50 as ₦15,000.50 by mistake, the customer may
see the wrong balance or have problems with transactions. That is why
information must be entered and stored correctly.


What I learned

Different information has different data types. Computers use these
types to handle information correctly.


Tier 5: How a Whole System Works


Project: How an Online Store Works
An online store lets people buy things using the internet. For example,
a customer may order two phones.


How the order works

1. Input: The customer chooses the phone, enters the quantity and provides delivery and payment details.
2. Program: The online store receives the information and manages  the order.
3. Instructions: The system checks the product, checks availability  and works out the price.
4. Processing: If one phone costs ₦50,000 and the customer orders two, the total is ₦100,000.
5. Stored data: The store saves the order details, such as thenproduct, quantity, total price, delivery address and payment status.
6. Output: The customer sees a message confirming that the order has been received.



1. What is the computer doing?

It receives the order, checks the details, calculates the total and
displays the result.


2. What is the program doing?

It gives the computer the instructions needed to handle the order.


3. What data is involved?

The customer's details, product, quantity, price, delivery address and
payment status.


4. What is the output?

The order confirmation shown to the customer.


5. Why does the store save information?

So the store can find the order later and know what the customer bought
and where it should be delivered.


What I learned


An online store uses information and instructions to complete an order.
The computer receives the customer's details, processes them, saves
important information and gives a result.


Final Summary


These five projects helped me understand how computers solve everyday
problems.

- Clear instructions explain exactly what needs to be done.
- An algorithm is a step-by-step way to solve a problem.
- Input is the information entered into a computer.
- Processing is the work the computer does with that information.
- Output is the result the computer gives.
- Data has different types, such as text, whole numbers, decimal numbers and true-or-false values.
- Programs give computers instructions, and stored data helps keep information for later use.

In simple terms: 
A computer takes information, follows instructions, works on the information and gives us a result.
